# Supplier Vetting Guide — Amazon FBA Wholesale Sourcing

Reference doc for AICC. Use before committing money to any new wholesale distributor or ungating supplier.

## Case study: Infinity Distribution LLC (Tomball, TX) — REJECTED

Flagged during research on 2026-07-03. Do not use.

- Website content (About, FAQs, testimonials) is word-for-word identical to premierproductscous.com ("Premier Products Co US") — a clone-site pattern typical of invoice mills.
- The clone twin (Premier Products Co US) has multiple Trustpilot complaints: sellers paid $3,000–$3,300, Amazon received empty/mismatched boxes, company stopped responding or blocked the buyer.
- No independent Trustpilot, BBB, or seller-forum history found for Infinity Distribution LLC itself.
- Their own Terms & Conditions state: *"we do not claim to be authorized distributors of any brand unless specifically stated"* and they take no responsibility for "Amazon or brand issues."
- All sales final, no refunds — meaning a rejected ungating application leaves you stuck with unsellable inventory.

## The three legitimate sourcing paths (ranked by safety)

1. **Brand-direct outreach** — safest. Contact the brand's wholesale/B2B department directly, ask to become an authorized reseller. Slower, sometimes needs a business license and MOQ, but the invoice is bulletproof.
2. **Established directories** — Wholesale Central, SaleHoo, Worldwide Brands. These are lists of vetted distributors, not distributors themselves. A one-time/annual directory fee is normal; a per-order "membership fee to unlock pricing" is not.
3. **Trade shows** — ASD Market Week, Global Sources, category-specific shows. Meet reps in person, verify on the spot.

## Verification checklist — run on every new distributor

- [ ] Confirm business registration via Texas Secretary of State (sos.state.tx.us) or the relevant state's registry
- [ ] Request a brand authorization letter, then call the brand directly to confirm it's real
- [ ] Search "[company name] + Trustpilot" and "+ reviews" — check dates, volume, and specificity of complaints
- [ ] Check if their About/testimonial copy matches another wholesale site verbatim (clone-site red flag)
- [ ] Review their refund/return policy — "all sales final" paired with an ungating pitch is a red flag
- [ ] Start with a small test order before a large commitment, even if MOQs are technically higher

## Red flags (auto-reject)

- Identical site content/testimonials to another wholesale site under a different name
- Terms explicitly disclaiming "authorized distributor" status
- No verifiable Trustpilot/BBB history despite claimed years in business
- Contact info limited to an email form, no address you can independently verify
- Prices significantly below market wholesale rates
- Pressure to pay via ACH/wire only, no buyer protection
